Purpose of the Assignment 
Core purpose: This assignment evaluates your defensible decision-making. You must be able to explain why you selected 
particular requirements, assumptions, tests, expected results, defects, and quality conclusions. Running the required tools 
is compulsory, but raw tool output or a high score is not sufficient without your own interpretation and evidence. 
Tools and Technology 
• Development technology: There is no restriction on programming language, frontend, backend, framework, or 
database. The completed application must run and implement the selected requirements.  
• AI-assisted coding: An AI-assisted coding/vibe-coding tool may be used. The pair remains responsible for the 
code and must record important assumptions introduced during development.  
• SonarQube (required): After the selected scope has been fully implemented and the baseline code has been 
frozen, the pair must run the complete codebase through SonarQube and submit the generated report/evidence. 
Do not scan only selected files or snippets. 
• Jira (required): Use Jira to record confirmed, reproducible software defects found through testing.  
Official setup and tutorial links: SonarQube Community Build download | SonarQube quick-start tutorial | Jira Free | Create 
a Jira work item 
Team and Project Selection 
• Complete the assignment in pairs (two students). 
• Cross section formulation of groups is not allowed. 
• Enter both students’ names/IDs in the project row on the Google Sheet tab assigned to your section.  
• Choose one of the 45 supplied SRSs by using a row marked Available. Within your section, each SRS may be 
assigned to only one pair; the same SRS may be chosen independently in another section. A complete entry in 
your section tab confirms the first-come, first-served selection. 
Requirement Selection Rules 
• Select exactly 10 requirements from the chosen SRS: 7 Functional Requirements (70%) and 3 Non -Functional 
Requirements (30%). Preserve the original requirement IDs and wording where possible.  
• No more than 3 of the 7 FRs may be pure CRUD requirements. 
• Complete management of one entity counts as one FR. For example, create, view, edit, and delete a student 
record together count as one ‘Manage Student Record’ FR—not four separate FRs. 
• The remaining FRs should represent meaningful behaviour such as business rules, validation, calculations, 
decisions, workflows, permissions, or interactions between features. 
Required Workflow 
1. Register the pair and project. Use only your section’s tab and complete the row for an Available SRS before 
another pair in the same section does. 
2. Select the scope. Choose 7 FRs and 3 NFRs using the rules above. 
3. Develop the application. Use an acceptable AI-assisted approach and implement the selected scope with an 
observable GUI. 
4. Freeze the baseline. Preserve the first complete runnable version that will be evaluated.  
5. Run SonarQube. Submit the complete frozen codebase to SonarQube and retain the generated report/evidence.  
6. Design and execute tests. Test all selected FRs, including manual system-level cases. 
7. Investigate and report defects. Confirm reproducible implementation defects before entering them in Jira.  
8. Conclude and defend. Use the collected evidence to reach a scoped quality judgment and explain all decisions 
during the demo/viva.

B. Functional Test Derivation, Execution and Traceability (30 marks)  
Derive test conditions for all 7 FRs, then design and execute 12–15 test cases. The full set must include normal 
behaviour, business rules, exact boundaries, and invalid/error conditions. Expected results must come from the SRS 
or another stated, defensible test basis. 
Minimum test-set requirements: 
• At least 2 test cases must target exact boundary values. 
• At least 2 test cases must target invalid input or error handling. 
• At least 3 test cases must be system-level tests executed manually by a person. Automation is not expected 
because a system-test automation tool has not been covered. A system-level case exercises the integrated 
application from a user-visible entry point to an observable end result; it is not a single function or isolated 
component test. 
• In total, include at least 2 non-trivial/complex test cases whose genuine execution status is FAILED or BLOCKED. 
These may be two FAILED cases, two BLOCKED cases, or one of each. Do not manufacture an outcome or 
relabel a PASSED case merely to meet this requirement. 
• A FAILED test was executed but the actual result did not match the expected result. A BLOCKED test could not be 
completed because a required precondition, dependency, environment, or earlier step prevented execution. 
Record the exact reason and evidence for either status. 
The same test may satisfy more than one category—for example, a manual system-level test may also cover an exact 
boundary—but you must label the relevant categories clearly.

Part 4 — Defect Reporting and Final Quality Judgment (15 marks) 
Investigate every FAILED or BLOCKED test before deciding whether it represents a defect. Check the test setup, data, 
environment, preconditions, and expected result; reproduce the problem where possible; and collect evidence. Log 
only confirmed, reproducible implementation defects in Jira. A test status and a defect are not the same thing, and not 
every failure or blocker should become a Jira bug. 
Each Jira defect must contain: 
• A short, specific title and the affected environment/build. 
• Preconditions, minimal reproduction steps, expected result, and actual result.  
• Reproducibility, severity, priority, supporting evidence, and the related test-case ID. 
• A suitable workflow status, if used in the team’s Jira project. 
Final quality judgment (300–400 words):  
State what the combined evidence supports, what remains unsupported, how AI -introduced assumptions affected 
confidence, and whether the evaluated 10-requirement scope can reasonably be considered acceptable. Your 
conclusion must be limited to the scope and evidence actually evaluated.  
Submission Checklist 
• Pair details and confirmed SRS selection from your section’s Google Sheet tab.  
• One report containing the 7 FR + 3 NFR scope table, AI assumptions, testing records, traceability, and the 300 –
400 word final judgment. 
• Frozen baseline source code, setup/run instructions, and brief AI-assisted development record. 
• Complete SonarQube report/evidence produced from the entire frozen codebase, plus interpretation of 
approximately five meaningful findings. 
• Evidence for evaluation of all 3 selected NFRs. 
• 12–15 executed test cases, including at least 2 boundary cases, at least 2 invalid/error cases, at least 3 manually 
executed system-level cases, and at least 2 non-trivial cases genuinely marked FAILED or BLOCKED. 
• Jira defect evidence/export for confirmed, reproducible defects. 
Demo/Viva Requirement 
Important deduction rule: Submission of files does not guarantee full marks. During the demo/viva, students must 
answer questions about the topics covered, the selected requirements, the code, the SonarQube report, the tests, the Jira 
defects, and the decisions made. Failure to explain these may result in deductions, regardless of what was submitted. ‘We 
forgot during the demo’ is not an acceptable excuse. Each member of the pair should understand the complete 
submission.
